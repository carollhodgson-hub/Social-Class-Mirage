Purpose of the Game

Most people have ideas about what it means to be “rich,” “middle class,” or “poor.” Television, social media, advertisements, and even families often create images of what life should look like at different class levels. Sociologists call this tension between what people say they value and what actually happens in daily life the difference between ideal culture and real culture.

Important Sociology Distinction: Income Is Not Wealth

Income is money received from work, business, or investments. Wealth is what people own after subtracting what they owe. A person with a high salary can still be financially vulnerable if they have large debts, high expenses, unstable work, or little savings. A wealthy household may have assets, investments, inherited resources, and social connections that continue creating opportunities even when no one is working a traditional job.

Reflection Questions

Instructor’s Sociology Note

Many Americans believe social class is determined only by individual choices, effort, or intelligence. Sociologists recognize that class position is also shaped by wealth, debt, inheritance, education, family background, social networks, health, discrimination, job opportunities, and unexpected life events.

The question is not whether emergencies happen. The question is how prepared people are to absorb them when they do.
